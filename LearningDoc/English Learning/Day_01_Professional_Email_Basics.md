# Day 1 — Professional Email Basics

**Goal for today:** Write clear, polite and confident emails to your manager and senior management.

> Note: You didn't add your job role, so the samples use a general office project. Simply replace the project names and details with your own work.

---

## 1. The Ideal Professional Email Structure

Think of every email as six small blocks. If each block is in place, your email will always look professional.

| Block | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Subject line** | Tells the reader what the email is about before they open it. Keep it short and specific. | `Weekly Status Update – Vendor Portal – 24 Oct` |
| **Greeting** | A respectful hello. | `Hi Rajesh,` / `Dear Ms. Mehta,` |
| **Opening line** | One sentence that says why you are writing. | `I'm writing to share this week's progress on the vendor portal.` |
| **Body** | The main information. Short paragraphs or bullet points. Facts first. | Completed tasks, next steps, risks |
| **Call to action** | What you need from the reader, and by when. | `Could you please approve the budget by Thursday?` |
| **Closing** | A polite ending + your name. | `Best regards,` / `Thank you,` |

**Quick tips**
- **Manager (daily contact):** "Hi [first name]," is fine in most companies.
- **Senior management / someone new:** Start with "Dear [name]," or "Hello [name]," and follow their style after they reply.
- **Bottom line up front:** Senior leaders are busy. Put the most important point in the first two lines.

## 2. Opening Lines & Closing Lines

### 10 Polite Opening Lines

1. I hope you're doing well.
2. I hope you had a great weekend.
3. I'm writing to update you on the progress of [project].
4. I'm reaching out regarding [topic].
5. Thank you for your quick response.
6. Following up on our conversation this morning, …
7. As discussed in yesterday's meeting, …
8. I wanted to bring to your attention that …
9. Please find attached the [report/document] for your review.
10. I wanted to give you a quick heads-up about …

### 10 Professional Closing Lines

1. Please let me know if you have any questions.
2. I look forward to your feedback.
3. Happy to discuss this further if needed.
4. Thank you for your time and support.
5. Please let me know if you need any further information.
6. I'll keep you posted on any updates.
7. I appreciate your guidance on this.
8. Looking forward to hearing from you.
9. Thank you in advance for your help.
10. I'm available for a quick call if that would be helpful.

**Sign-offs (last word before your name):**
`Best regards,` · `Kind regards,` · `Thank you,` · `Thanks and regards,` · `Best,` (informal, only with your own manager)

## 3. Five Complete Sample Emails

### (a) Weekly Status Update to Manager

**Subject:** Weekly Status Update – Vendor Portal Project – Week of 20 Oct

Hi Rajesh,

I hope you're doing well. Please find below this week's update on the vendor portal project.

**Completed this week**
- Finalized the login and registration module.
- Resolved 12 out of 15 open issues from testing.
- Shared the user guide draft with the support team.

**Planned for next week**
- Fix the remaining 3 issues.
- Begin integration with the payment system.

**Risks / blockers**
- We are still waiting for API access from the finance team. If we don't receive it by Wednesday, the integration may slip by two days.

Could you please follow up with the finance team lead on the API access? It would really help us stay on track.

Please let me know if you have any questions.

Best regards,
Amit

---

### (b) Project Completion Update to Senior Management

**Subject:** Vendor Portal Successfully Launched – Summary

Dear Ms. Mehta,

I'm pleased to inform you that the vendor portal went live on 24 October, as planned.

**Key highlights**
- Delivered on schedule and within the approved budget.
- 85 vendors registered in the first two days.
- Invoice processing time is expected to reduce by around 40%.

**Next steps**
- We will monitor performance for the next two weeks and share a usage report on 7 November.

I would like to thank the finance and support teams for their excellent collaboration throughout the project.

Please let me know if you would like a detailed walkthrough. I would be happy to set up a short session at your convenience.

Kind regards,
Amit Patel
Project Lead – Vendor Portal

---

### (c) Sharing a Delay Early

**Subject:** Heads-up: Possible Delay in Payment Integration

Hi Rajesh,

I wanted to give you an early heads-up about a possible delay in the payment integration.

The finance team has informed us that API access will only be available next Monday instead of this Wednesday. Because of this, the integration is likely to move from **28 October to 31 October**.

**To minimize the impact, I plan to:**
- Complete all other testing in the meantime.
- Prepare the integration code in advance, so we can start as soon as access is ready.

The overall launch date is not affected at this stage. I will keep you posted if anything changes.

Please let me know if you would prefer a different approach.

Thanks and regards,
Amit

### (d) Thanking a Senior for Support

**Subject:** Thank You for Your Support on the Vendor Portal

Dear Mr. Shah,

Your quick approval of the additional testing resources made a real difference. It helped us resolve critical issues on time and launch without any major problems.

### (e) Introducing Myself to a New Senior Manager

**Subject:** Introduction – Amit Patel, Project Lead, Vendor Portal

Dear Ms. Kapoor,

I hope you're doing well. Welcome to the team!

My name is Amit Patel, and I am the project lead for the vendor portal. I have been with the company for four years, and I currently lead a team of five. Our main focus is improving the vendor payment process.

I would be glad to give you a brief overview of our current projects, key results and upcoming priorities. Would you have 20 minutes sometime next week for a short introductory meeting?

I look forward to working with you.

Best regards,
Amit Patel
Project Lead – Vendor Portal

## 4. Weak vs Strong Phrases

| ❌ Avoid (weak) | ✅ Use instead (strong) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Sorry to bother you… | Thank you for your time… | You are not a disturbance; your work matters. |
| Sorry for the late reply. | Thank you for your patience. | Positive instead of apologetic. |
| Just checking if you saw my email. | Following up on my email from Monday regarding… | Clear and professional. |
| I think maybe we could… | I recommend that we… | Sounds confident and decisive. |
| I'm not sure, but… | Based on my analysis… | Shows you did your homework. |
| I'll try to finish it. | I will finish it by Friday. | A clear commitment builds trust. |
| Does that make sense? | Please let me know if you have any questions. | Doesn't doubt your own explanation. |
| ASAP | by 3 PM tomorrow | A specific deadline is clearer and more polite. |
| I have a small problem. | I'd like to flag an issue. | Neutral, professional tone. |
| Please do the needful. | Could you please approve the request? | Says exactly what action you need. |
| Kindly revert. | I look forward to your reply. | "Revert" is unclear outside India. |
| Hope you understand. | I appreciate your understanding. | Sounds more mature and respectful. |

## 5. Five Common Email Mistakes That Look Unprofessional

1. **A vague or empty subject line.** "Hi" or "Update" tells the reader nothing. Use specific details, for example: `Approval Needed – Training Budget – by 30 Oct`.
2. **A very long first paragraph.** Seniors may read only the first two lines. Put the key message or request at the top.
3. **No clear call to action.** If you need something, say what and by when. Avoid ending with only "Please check."
4. **Writing when you are angry or emotional.** Capital letters, many exclamation marks (!!!) or sarcasm can damage relationships. Wait an hour, then reply calmly.
5. **Not checking before sending.** Look for wrong names, missing attachments ("please find attached" but no file), and the wrong people in "Reply All". Read your email once aloud before you click Send.

## 6. ✅ Golden Rules of Professional Email (Checklist)

Check these before you click **Send**. If every box is ticked, your email is ready.

**Before writing**
- [ ] I know the **one main purpose** of this email (inform, request, or confirm).
- [ ] I am **calm**. If I feel angry or upset, I will wait at least one hour.
- [ ] Email is the right channel. (For urgent or sensitive issues, call or meet first, then confirm by email.)

**Subject line**
- [ ] It is **short and specific**: topic + action + deadline if needed.
  *Example: `Approval Needed – Training Budget – by 30 Oct`*
- [ ] It is not empty, and not just "Hi" or "Update".

**Greeting & opening**
- [ ] I used the person's **correct name** and spelling.
- [ ] The greeting fits the person: "Hi [name]," for my manager; "Dear [name]," for senior or new contacts.
- [ ] My **first two lines** say why I am writing.

**Body**
- [ ] **One topic per email.** A different topic gets a separate email.
- [ ] Short paragraphs (2–3 lines) or bullet points.
- [ ] **Facts, numbers and dates** instead of feelings or guesses.
- [ ] Bad news is shared **early**, together with my plan to fix it.
- [ ] No slang, no SMS language ("pls", "u", "thx"), and no emojis with seniors.
- [ ] No ALL CAPS, no "!!!", no sarcasm.

**Call to action**
- [ ] It is clear **what** I need, **from whom**, and **by when**.
- [ ] I used a specific deadline instead of "ASAP".

**Tone**
- [ ] Polite but confident: "Thank you for your patience", not "Sorry, sorry".
- [ ] Strong words: "I will", "I recommend", not "I'll try", "maybe".
- [ ] I wrote nothing I would be uncomfortable seeing forwarded to the CEO.

**Closing**
- [ ] A polite closing line + sign-off + my name (and title for senior emails).

**Final check (30 seconds)**
- [ ] Attachment is actually attached.
- [ ] The **To / CC / BCC** recipients are correct. I avoided an unnecessary "Reply All".
- [ ] I read the email **once out loud**. It sounds respectful and clear.
- [ ] Spell-check done.

**Three golden habits**
1. **Reply within 24 hours**, even if only to say: *"Thank you, I'll get back to you by Thursday."*
2. **Confirm verbal discussions in writing**: *"As discussed in today's meeting, …"*. This protects you later.
3. **Shorter is stronger.** If an email is longer than one screen, consider a quick call instead.

## 8. ⭐ Top 5 Phrases to Memorize Today

1. **"I'm writing to update you on…"** — ready-made start for any update email.
   *હું તમને … વિશે માહિતી આપવા લખી રહ્યો છું.*
2. **"I wanted to give you an early heads-up about…"** — share bad news early, calmly.
   *હું તમને … વિશે અગાઉથી જાણ કરવા માંગતો હતો.*
3. **"Could you please [action] by [day/time]?"** — a clear, polite call to action.
   *શું તમે કૃપા કરીને [દિવસ/સમય] સુધીમાં [કામ] કરી શકશો?*
4. **"I'll keep you posted."** — shows ownership and builds trust.
   *હું તમને માહિતગાર રાખીશ.*
5. **"Thank you for your patience."** — replace "Sorry for the late reply" forever.
   *તમારી ધીરજ બદલ આભાર.*
